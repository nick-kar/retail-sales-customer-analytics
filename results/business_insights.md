# Verified business findings (synthetic data)

These figures were provided from SQL query results executed in PostgreSQL 18. They are **not** real commercial outcomes.

- **2025 completed-order revenue:** €94,889,714, across 90,000 completed orders. Revenue is `SUM(quantity * unit_price)` for completed orders only.
- **Highest-revenue month:** October 2025 (€8,093,793). **Lowest:** February 2025 (€7,360,329). The apparent monthly pattern is generated by deterministic date formulas and is **not** evidence of seasonality.
- **Purchasing customers:** 9,000; **repeat purchasers:** 9,000 (100% of purchasing customers). This is a **generator artifact**, not a realistic retention finding. The other 1,000 registered customers did not have completed purchases.
- **Highest-revenue category leader:** Product 27 (Home), €1,017,491, 5.40% of its category's revenue. Other category leaders are documented in `top_products.csv`.

## Query-performance experiment

- Filter: completed orders dated July 1, 2025 or later, ordered by date; 45,475 rows returned.
- Before index: sequential scan plus sort; **24.128 ms** (one run).
- After composite B-tree index on `(order_status, order_date)`: index scan; **22.601, 20.298, 16.347, 18.017, 20.568 ms** (five runs). Median **20.298 ms**, numerically **15.87% below** the single baseline.
- **Limitation:** This is not a controlled speedup estimate. There is only one pre-index observation, and caching, execution order, and runtime variability may explain part of the difference. The index also resulted in far more shared buffer hits.
